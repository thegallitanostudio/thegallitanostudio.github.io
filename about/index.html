About Angela Gallitano

I learned to read a room long before I ever sat in a boardroom.

Most people spend their careers learning how to perform. I spent mine learning to read what’s underneath the performance: the thing left unsaid, the person the room is waiting on, the story sitting behind the title.

By the time I was across the table from CHROs and executive teams, I saw people first. Brilliant leaders doing their very best inside systems built around their titles.

And people could tell. So they told me things.

01 · The search

I took the long way, and it was the right one.

Three colleges. Seven majors. I was chasing one question from every angle I could find: how do people actually work?

Linguistics. Nutrition sciences. Cultural anthropology. Pre-med. Even Ancient Greek. Zoology won. (It has been far more useful in boardrooms than anyone warned me.)

I was looking for what makes people open up, and what finally gets them to move.

02 · The arena

The real story lives off the org chart.

For more than ten years I sat with CHROs and HR executives at some of the largest organizations in the country: Fortune 500 manufacturers and logistics companies, major health systems and health plans. Together we worked through AI strategy, CEO succession and board presentations.

It was always about listening. Asking the question others missed, and noticing which executive everyone was quietly following.

People told me the real story. (And I mean the REAL story.) I used it only to help: shaping my guidance to each situation, seeking out other perspectives, and keeping every client’s name private.

Customers tell me they’re thinking about leaving before they tell their boss. Executives call me about the conversation they can only have outside their own company.

That happens because they’ve watched what I do with what they tell me.

03 · The name

This work existed long before it had a name.

For years, people pulled me aside. A founder explaining what their company actually does. An executive rewriting their story before a board meeting. A leader finding the words for the first time.

350+ rooms. 125+ executives one to one. The US, Italy, the UK, Hong Kong and beyond. After sessions, in hallways, on calls that ran long, inside other companies’ programs. I’m proud of that work.

It’s still happening. A few months into a new company, I asked for thirty minutes with the one person who could say yes. Thirty minutes later, we had a commitment.

Find who the room is waiting on. Ask.

At LinkedIn

Nominated as one of three Rock Your Profile leaders worldwide. I oversaw and trained the global program.

Then I noticed I’d been helping everyone else write their story. So I gave the work a name, and put mine on it.

Built for leaders who are done performing.

At some point the title, the leaderboard and the applause stop being enough. You want to lead as yourself.

That’s usually when people call me. We find the story underneath, shape it in your own words, and make sure the leader people meet online and in the room is the one you actually are.

Stop auditioning. Be known for who you actually are.
