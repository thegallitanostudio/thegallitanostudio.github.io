How I coach it · For the leader in the seat

Hands-on AI advisory · Inside your approved tools

You have to get in. You have to fail.

The best AI use cases are the most personal ones. So we start with where your week actually breaks, not with a tool.

The part nobody says out loud

Over the last two years, executives have learned the language of AI: governance, risk, the board strategy. Behind closed doors, very few are using it themselves.

If you’re approving AI roadmaps on second-hand summaries, you aren’t leading from the front. You aren’t leading it at all.

I get it. The expectations are unforgiving. You’re supposed to have the answers to a shift nobody was trained on. You don’t need another white paper. You need a confidential room where you can sit down, roll up your sleeves, break it, and build something that gives you your week back.

Where we start

Friction rarely shows up where the vendors say it does. It shows up in how you actually work:

  • Saying yes to everything, until the calendar collapses.
  • To-dos scattered across notebooks, sticky notes and half-written emails.
  • A task that should take twenty minutes taking two hours by hand, because you don’t know where to start.

We sit down inside the tools your company has already cleared. We find your baseline, look at what you’re still doing by hand, and ask the question most leaders haven’t built the muscle to ask yet: is this an automation candidate?

What fluency looks like

Fluency is not “did you know it could do that?”

Fluency is knowing which tool to reach for, and putting it down the second it stops serving you. It’s agents running in the background while you keep hold of the story.

AI leadership also means letting go of the old org chart. There’s an intern in your company right now who runs these models fluently. The leaders who get this don’t gatekeep it behind titles. They find the super users, build tiger teams, hold open office hours, and let the best workflow win.

Six weeks in

We go at your pace. We start at the foundation and build until your week runs differently:

  • Running in the background.Prompts and agents doing the research, the synthesis and the first drafts, at work and at home.
  • Your voice, intact.Every email and every board-prep document sounds like you. Never like a chatbot.
  • Steady in the room.You stop reacting to the hype cycle. You walk into the boardroom, the executive committee, the investor meeting knowing what works, what fails, and how to direct it.
